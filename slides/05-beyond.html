"So how do you make the check happen in a team? Two kinds of review, and one hard gate."

"First, the AI review. Let it take the mechanical findings: the missing null check, the unused import, the query without an index. It is fast, and it doesn't get bored."

"Then the human. Judgment. Is this the right change? Does it do what the ticket actually asked for? That is the part you can't hand off."

"And the gate. If either review is missing, the merge stops. [pause] That happened to me. My GitHub billing limit ran out, the AI review quietly didn't run, and the pull requests merged anyway. Nothing was red. It just shrugged. A missing check has to be a stop, not a shrug."

"Loops catch a lot. Atlassian puts it at about half of potential vulnerabilities. But only if the loop actually runs."

"The gate stops a bad merge. But a merge is not the finish line. Done means verified, not merged."

"So Done is four columns. Spec: every scenario has a test, including the edge case. Reviewed: the AI review ran, and its findings were resolved. [point at Verified] Verified: a human checked the real system. Only then, Done. The ticket closes here, not at the merge."

If I led engineering

Redesigning Jobs Around Judgment.

Agents generate. People decide. Leaders design that system.

Juniors

  • Learn in the loop: specs, plans, and review.
  • Use agents to see more patterns, not to skip deciding Done.
  • Skipping the loop skips the craft.

Seniors

  • Own taste in the repo: architecture, constraints, harness.
  • Review and verification are the scarce craft.
  • Own orchestration: hand-offs, bots, and what reaches a human.

Leaders

  • Set capacity on specs and review, not typing speed.
  • Make one loop the default across teams.
  • Protect the junior pipeline. Demand judgment in writing.

60: 1:30.

"I was VP Engineering at Joyn. About 55 people, six teams, about 11 million monthly users. If I led engineering at a company like ProSiebenSat.1 today, I would not chase typing speed. I would redesign the jobs around judgment."

"Juniors: the loop is the apprenticeship. Specs, plans, and review. Agents let them see more patterns. They must not skip deciding what Done means. Skip the loop, and you skip the craft."

"Seniors: less time writing every line, more time on architecture, constraints, the harness, and review. Taste has to live in the repo. And they own orchestration: which bots run, how work hands off, and what still reaches a human."

"And the VP job: set capacity on how many specs you can write and how many pull requests you can review well. One loop across teams. Protect how juniors grow. Demand that senior judgment is written down. Agents generate. People decide. Leaders design that system."
